# Strange Heroes: compact play reference

An original, unofficial summary of **Strange Heroes Playtest v1.02**, Tate
Renforth / Monken8. Citations use the consulted 94-page PDF's page numbers.
Get the rules from the [creator](https://monken8.itch.io/strange-heroes-playtest).
Consult your own edition for exceptions; this aid neither replaces talent text
nor claims complete rules automation.

## Checks and outcomes

For an uncertain task, roll **3d6**, add the applicable attribute and other
bonuses/penalties, then compare with a **Target Check (TC)**. STRANGE means
Strength, Tenacity, Reflexes, Acuity, Nexus, Grit and Empathy. A flat check ignores
bonuses/penalties. An action check spends its stated actions; a basic check
normally does not. Saving throws normally do not spend actions. (pp. 7–10)

Normal TC is **10 + tier**. Relative difficulty adjustments are easy −4, simple
−2, challenging +2, hard +4 and incredibly hard +6. Warn the player before an
impossible attempt: it produces perfect failure without awarding frustration.
Round fractions down unless a specific rule says otherwise. (pp. 8–9)

Resolve degree from the final check total and dice:

* Failure below TC; spectacular failure at least six below.
* All three dice showing ones, with a failing total, give perfect failure.
* Success above TC; spectacular success at least six above.
* All ones **or** all sixes, with a successful total, give perfect success.

A triple is therefore not an automatic success regardless of modifiers. The
book's p. 7 leaves equality unassigned, whereas defence text on p. 29 allows
meeting the defence. **Declare a tie ruling before rolling.** Strange Forge's
recommended campaign ruling is meet-or-beat; record it explicitly, rather than
claiming the p. 7 wording is unambiguous. The [creator's interview](https://gmshoe.wordpress.com/2024/07/10/qa-tate-renforth-strange-heroes/) also describes equal-or-higher success. (pp. 7, 29)

**Confidence** permits chosen dice rerolls; the player must identify their
choices. With stacked confidence, p. 9 describes sequential rerolls and permits
rerolling the same die again. Clarify the exact allowance before the check.
**Doubt** requires rerolling the current highest die, choosing between ties;
stacked doubt repeats this sequentially. An all-ones result with doubt reduces
the degree by one. Confidence and doubt cancel one-for-one. They are not a
second whole roll with "take the best/worst." (p. 9)

## Resources and character basics

Health is **30 × Tenacity**, or 10 if Tenacity is zero or negative. Energy is
**30 × Grit**, or 10 if Grit is zero or negative. Attribute defences are
**10 + the attribute**. Character building normally awards five attribute points,
five talent selections and two features per tier; the attribute investment cap
is tier +3. Nexus is not bought or reduced with ordinary attribute points.
Source/Training can supply additional features, tags and required talent choices. (pp. 10–11, 28–30)

A failed PC check awards one **frustration**, normally capped at three; Nexus
increases that cap. Initiative clears frustration, then combat start grants the
Nexus amount. Spend frustration before a check for confidence; at zero health
it can fund an immediate recovery roll; a minute's rest permits recovery of
30 HP or 10 EN per spent frustration. Verify timing and allied-recovery exceptions. (pp. 12, 22, 29)

Spectacular/perfect PC successes award the GM one **Ego**, including outside
combat. Ego has no pool maximum and clears at session end. Its uses include
doubt before another character's check, ending a condition at turn start, or
an additional action at a character's turn end. Supervillain-tagged characters
may use at most three Ego per round; this is not a three-point pool cap.
(pp. 5, 7, 12)

## Combat and damage

Confirm positions; initiative is **1d6 + Grit**. Tied NPCs precede PCs; tied PCs
choose their order. At turn start the ordinary allocation is **three actions and
one reaction**. Quick actions cost one; extended actions spend the listed amount;
reactions need their trigger. Preparing a quick action costs two actions and
later uses the reaction on the announced trigger. (pp. 15–16)

Each attack check imposes a further **−3** on subsequent attack checks until
the attacker's next turn, accumulating to **−9**. Missing still counts.
A basic melee attack uses Strength against Strength defence; a ranged weapon
uses Reflexes against Reflexes defence. Talent attacks can differ. (pp. 18–19)

Identify one check die as the damage die. Normal damage is that die multiplied
by tier, then the associated attribute added; talents may alter the multiplier.
For spectacular damage, add one chosen non-damage check die to the damage die
before multiplying. Perfect damage can add both, and can push. Damage Reduction
subtracts its rating multiplied by the defender's tier for the applicable
health/energy damage. (pp. 8, 21–22)

HP bottoms out at zero; EN can become negative. At negative maximum EN the
character is exhausted, with stunned/stupefied consequences until EN exceeds
zero. Zero HP, death, pulling punches and revival need the specific p. 22 rule;
do not silently equate all zero-HP results with ordinary unconsciousness. (p. 22)

## Powers: look up, commit, resolve

For each selected talent, look up its **prerequisites, action requirement,
cost, duration, range and effect**. Pay printed HP/EN costs directly: damage
reduction does not discount them. Extended activations require consecutive
actions; interruptions can lose progress. Higher numbered talents supersede
lower-level overlapping effects. Non-core sets ordinarily require an unlock
selection. Concentration timing needs the entry plus pp. 18 and 34; those pages
differ on extending one effect versus all talents, so record a ruling. (pp. 30, 34)

Record selected powers with page references. Search the private index by name,
inspect the page, state targets/costs before rolling, then commit changes once.
Never invent a cost or claim the ledger checks prerequisites.
