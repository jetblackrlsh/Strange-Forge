# A small conversational GM procedure

Use the user's own book and a mutually agreed campaign profile. Fiction first;
state changes must follow what was actually resolved, not what was merely
suggested. This is an adjudication workflow, not a promise that an AI never errs.

## Before play

1. Agree on tone, boundaries, tier and the scope of the scene.
2. Guide character creation conversationally: concept → tier → STRANGE attributes
   → Source/Training → talents/features → derived values and details. Consult
   pp. 28–34 rather than inventing a class menu. Ask one useful choice at a time.
3. Check prerequisites, purchase budgets and derived values in the actual book.
   Store selected talent names and page references, not a copied talent catalog.
4. Announce tie policy, any confidence-reroll interpretation and other house
   rulings. Record whether Nexus is zero or its increased frustration cap applies.
5. Load and validate the campaign. Summarize current resources, unresolved
   conditions, scene clocks and pending transactions so the player can correct
   discrepancies before play.

## One resolution loop

1. **Intent:** Ask what the hero wants to accomplish and how. If success is
   assured and no meaningful uncertainty exists, narrate without a gratuitous
   roll. If impossible, explain that before commitment (p. 9).
2. **Look up only what matters:** Read the applicable core rule and the selected
   talent's actual entry. Cite edition and page. Verify ownership/prerequisites,
   target, range/line of effect, action type, duration, health/energy price and
   any talent-specific check or saving throw (pp. 8, 16–19, 21–22, 34 and talent page).
   If evidence is missing, say so; propose a temporary ruling rather than
   pretending the book contains one.
3. **Explain before rolling:** State legal options, costs, TC/defence, attribute,
   modifiers, confidence/doubt, repeated-attack penalty and chosen ambiguity
   policy. Ask for commitment when the player has a meaningful choice.
4. **Roll transparently:** Use the cryptographic roller. Preserve dice faces,
   expression and total in a receipt. Checks normally use 3d6 plus relevant
   modifiers (p. 7); initiative is a different roll (p. 12). Resolve selected
   confidence rerolls and mandatory doubt rerolls from p. 9 explicitly. An attack
   must identify its damage die before damage is calculated (p. 8).
5. **Resolve, then narrate:** Determine degree using p. 7 and the campaign tie
   ruling; apply talent exceptions, damage, saves and conditions from their
   cited sources. Describe a consequence consistent with the result. Ordinary
   failure awards PC frustration; spectacular/perfect PC success awards GM Ego
   even outside combat (pp. 7, 12). Respect the cap and impossible-task exception.
6. **Commit once:** Propose the complete before/after numerical transaction.
   Include paid costs, action/reaction use, repeat-attack tracking, target
   health/energy, frustration/Ego, conditions, inventory, gold and clocks as
   relevant. Only mark it completed when applied successfully. A proposal alone
   cannot spend energy or transfer an item. Use a unique transaction identity
   so a repeated message does not apply the same consequence twice.
7. **Save and recap:** Validate, atomically save, and report remaining resources
   and outstanding decisions. On failure, retain the prior valid save and say
   the update was not persisted. Never narrate a successful save without evidence.

## Combat guardrails

At turn start, the ordinary profile grants three actions and one reaction
(p. 16); individual effects can alter this. Each attack check adds a further
−3 penalty to later attack checks until the attacker's next turn, capped at −9
(p. 19). A missed attack still counts. A reaction needs its trigger; an extended
action must pay its required consecutive actions. Talent costs come from the
specific entry and are not reduced as if they were incoming damage (p. 34).

Keep concentration, multi-turn activation, trigger timing, duration and condition
interpretation visible to the GM. The ledger is not a tactical validator: it
cannot infer legal targeting or catch every missed prerequisite. Do not invent
an energy cost merely because an effect sounds powerful.

## Recovery and trust

Use a local backup after a corrupt save; validate it before restoring. Do not
silently reset or "repair" unknown state. Distinguish pending choices, proposed
transactions, completed changes and failed writes. Never upload a private
campaign or book index to publish a code release. Images and documents can be
created through tools the host already has, but they are optional presentation;
this project supplies no provider API, cloud sync or free-generation promise.
